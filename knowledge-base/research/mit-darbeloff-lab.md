## Background context (web research)

**The d'Arbeloff Lab.** The d'Arbeloff Laboratory for Information Systems and Technology was established in 1995 within MIT's Department of Mechanical Engineering, funded by a donation from Brit and Alex d'Arbeloff (Alex d'Arbeloff co-founded Teradyne). Per MIT News and the lab's own site, it is a cross-disciplinary research group spanning robotics, system dynamics and control, actuators, health monitoring, and biological systems — the thread tying these together is treating "information" (sensing, estimation, feedback) as a design element in mechanical systems. The lab has long been directed by Harry Asada, a professor of mechanical engineering known for work like Supernumerary Robotic Limbs (SRLs), wearable robotic arms that act autonomously alongside a human's own limbs. Karl's project — reading human force/intent and turning it into language, and using that to drive physical rehabilitation-adjacent robotics — fits squarely within the lab's long-running interest in human-robot physical interaction and health monitoring.

**RSS (Robotics: Science and Systems).** RSS is one of the three top-tier general robotics research conferences, alongside ICRA (IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation) and IROS (IEEE/RSJ International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems), with CoRL (Conference on Robot Learning) as a newer, learning-focused peer. Per conference statistics aggregators, RSS 2025 accepted 163 of 594 submissions — about 27.4% — and historically RSS acceptance rates have run from roughly 20–40%, generally tighter than ICRA (which runs closer to 40%) and IROS (which has recently run in the 36–47% range). RSS is also singly-reviewed as a single-track, highly selective venue rather than a sprawling multi-track conference, which contributes to its reputation as one of the most selective and prestigious venues in robotics research — being first author on an RSS paper as an undergraduate researcher is a notable achievement.

**Admittance control / virtual dynamics controllers.** In physical human-robot interaction, admittance control is a scheme where the robot's commanded motion is computed from a measured external force, via a virtual mechanical model — typically a mass-spring-damper system (the classic formulation is M·ẍ + D·ẋ + K·x = F_external). Instead of behaving like a rigid, purely position-controlled machine, the robot acts as if it has adjustable virtual inertia, damping, and stiffness, so that when a person pushes on it, it yields and moves compliantly rather than fighting back. This is the standard technique for making arms safe and intuitive to interact with physically, including in physical therapy and assistive robotics contexts, which is directly relevant to Karl's Spaulding collaboration.

**Universal Robots (UR) arms.** Universal Robots is a Danish manufacturer of 6-axis ("6 degree of freedom") collaborative robot arms — cobots — such as the UR3e, UR5e, and UR10e, distinguished by relatively easy programming, ROS compatibility, and built-in safety features that let them operate near humans without caging, which is why they are common in both industrial and academic robotics labs.

**Spaulding Rehabilitation Hospital.** Spaulding is a rehabilitation hospital system that is part of Mass General Brigham (the health system formed by Massachusetts General Hospital and Brigham and Women's Hospital) and a teaching hospital of Harvard Medical School. Its main Boston campus sits in the Charlestown Navy Yard. Per Mass General Brigham's own materials, Spaulding is consistently ranked among the best rehabilitation hospitals in the country by U.S. News & World Report (continuously since 1995, including a #2 national ranking in 2026-27), making it a natural clinical partner for a physical-therapy-adjacent, sensor-driven research study like Karl's.
