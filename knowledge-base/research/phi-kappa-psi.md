## Background context (web research)

**IRS Form 990.** Per IRS guidance, most organizations recognized as tax-exempt — including many student and campus organizations that have obtained 501(c) nonprofit status — must file an annual informational return with the IRS, generally due by the 15th day of the 5th month after their accounting year ends. Which specific version of the form applies depends on size: organizations with less than $50,000 in gross receipts can file the short Form 990-N; those under $200,000 in receipts and $500,000 in total assets can file the simplified Form 990-EZ; organizations above those thresholds must file the full Form 990. This filing discloses revenue, expenses, and organizational information to maintain the group's tax-exempt status — even an organization with no net "profit" motive still has to file to stay in good standing. A fraternity chapter treasurer managing on the order of $185,000–$200,000 per semester in member dues is operating well past the $50,000 threshold, which is consistent with Karl's description of filing 990 paperwork as part of the treasurer role (whether the specific form used was the EZ version or the full 990 was not independently verified in this research).
